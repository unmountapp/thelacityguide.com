Nigeria to LA 2028

Nigeria has emerged as a major force in track and field, basketball, and football. For LA 2028, Nigerian fans and athletes' families should plan their US visa well in advance.

⚠ Visa and entry requirements change frequently. Verify at your government's official travel authority and the US Embassy website. Last updated: June 2026.
← All International Guides

Entry Requirements

Nigerian citizens require a B-1/B-2 tourist visa to visit the United States. Nigeria is not part of the Visa Waiver Program. Apply at the nearest US Embassy (Lagos or Abuja).

⚠ US visa demand from Nigeria is very high. Processing wait times at the US Embassy can be 12–24+ months depending on appointment availability. Begin the application process as early as possible — ideally 18–24 months before your planned travel date for the 2028 Games.

Applying for a US B-1/B-2 Visa

  • Apply at the US Embassy in Lagos (Mission Street) or Abuja
  • Complete the DS-160 online form first
  • Pay the MRV (Machine Readable Visa) fee ($185 USD)
  • Schedule interview appointment — this is where the long waits occur
  • Bring: passport, DS-160 confirmation, photo, bank statements, employment letter, hotel/event booking confirmation

Flights

Direct flights from Lagos (LOS) to the US are operated by United and Delta (via connection). Most Nigerian travellers connect through London Heathrow, Amsterdam, Paris Charles de Gaulle, or Doha. Total travel time to LAX: typically 16–22 hours depending on routing.

Airlines to check: British Airways via LHR, KLM via AMS, Air France via CDG, Qatar Airways via DOH, Ethiopian Airlines via ADD, Turkish Airlines via IST.

Nigerian Communities in LA

Los Angeles has a growing Nigerian diaspora, particularly concentrated in the Inglewood, Carson, and Los Angeles proper areas. Nigerian restaurants, churches, and community organizations operate throughout the city.

Nigerian Athletes at the 2028 Olympics

Team Nigeria has shown strength in Athletics (sprints, hurdles), Basketball (Basketball Africa League development), Weightlifting, and Football. Following Nigerian athletes is one of the most exciting parts of attending the 2028 Games.

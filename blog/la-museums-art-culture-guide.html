Best Museums in LA: Art, Science & Culture Guide

From the hilltop Getty to the tar pits on Wilshire — LA's museum landscape is extraordinary.

Los Angeles is not typically the first city that comes to mind when people think of great museums. It should be. The Getty Center is one of the finest art institutions in the world and charges no admission. LACMA is the largest art museum west of Chicago. The Natural History Museum contains one of the most significant paleontology collections anywhere. The California Science Center houses a retired Space Shuttle. This is a museum city of the highest order, and the proximity to Olympic venues makes several of them genuinely convenient to work into your schedule.

The Essential: Getty Center

The Getty Center

FREE Admission
📍 Brentwood / West LA 🚌 Metro Rapid 761 from Westwood/UCLA 🕐 Tue–Sun 10am–5:30pm (Sat to 9pm)
Why It's Essential

The Getty Center is one of the great surprises of Los Angeles. Sitting on a hilltop above the 405 freeway in Brentwood, the Richard Meier-designed complex contains a world-class art collection, gardens with panoramic views of the city and the Pacific, and a restaurant with one of the best lunch views in LA — all completely free of charge (parking is $25). J. Paul Getty, the oil billionaire, endowed it with an absurd amount of money; the result is an institution that can acquire anything and admit everyone.

The Collection

European painting from the Middle Ages through the 19th century: Van Gogh's Irises, Monet's series paintings, Rembrandt, Rubens, Titian. Pre-Raphaelites, illuminated manuscripts, decorative arts. The Photographs department has one of the best collections in the world — the history of the medium from its origins to the contemporary. The Getty Villa in Pacific Palisades (a separate site, also free) houses the antiquities collection: Greek, Roman, and Etruscan art.

Practical Notes

The tram from the parking structure to the museum complex runs continuously and takes 5 minutes. Go on a weekday morning to avoid the largest crowds. The Central Garden by Robert Irwin is worth an hour on its own — a constantly changing seasonal planting designed as a work of art. The outdoor spaces are genuinely excellent and make the Getty as much a landscape experience as an art experience.

Museum Row: Wilshire Boulevard

A two-mile stretch of Wilshire Blvd between Fairfax and La Brea contains three major museums within walking distance of each other. A Metro E Line stop (Wilshire/La Brea) puts you at the center of the cluster. This is the most concentrated museum experience in Los Angeles.

LACMA (Los Angeles County Museum of Art)

📍 Mid-Wilshire / Museum Row 🚇 Metro E Line: Wilshire/La Brea 💰 $25 adults / under 18 free
The Scale

The largest art museum west of Chicago: 150,000+ works spanning 6,000 years. The scope is extraordinary — Chinese and Korean ceramics, South Asian sculpture, Islamic art (one of the best collections outside the Islamic world), European Old Masters, American painting, and a contemporary wing that includes Basquiat, Koons, Hockney, and Cindy Sherman. A full visit is a full day; plan which departments matter most and focus there.

Urban Light Installation

The outdoor installation of 202 antique cast-iron streetlamps by Chris Burden is free to visit at any time. It's one of the most-photographed locations in Los Angeles, particularly beautiful at dusk and after dark when the lamps glow amber. This is the thing you'll recognize from countless films and music videos. Access it from the Wilshire Blvd side — you don't need a museum ticket.

Levitated Mass

Michael Heizer's 340-ton granite boulder, suspended over a sunken pathway on the museum campus, is another permanent outdoor artwork included with admission (or viewable from outside for free). Walking below it creates one of the more uncanny experiences in the city.

La Brea Tar Pits & Page Museum

📍 Next to LACMA on Wilshire 💰 $17 adults / $10 children

One of the most surreal attractions in Los Angeles: active, bubbling asphalt pits in the middle of a major urban park, from which paleontologists have excavated over one million ice age fossils. The sticky natural tar that pooled here 10,000 to 40,000 years ago trapped and preserved dire wolves, mammoths, American lions, short-faced bears, and saber-toothed cats. The Page Museum adjacent to the pits displays the cleaned and reconstructed skeletons. Glass-walled laboratories let you watch paleontologists currently working on newly excavated bones. This is a singular, excellent, and strange LA experience that most visitors skip.

The Petersen Automotive Museum

📍 Across from LACMA on Wilshire 💰 $20–25 adults

The most visited automotive museum in the world, and worthy of the claim. The collection includes the original Batmobile, Herbie the Love Bug, James Dean's 550 Spyder, the most significant cars in film and television history, and serious exhibition space on automotive design, racing history, and the future of transportation. The building itself is one of the most dramatic in LA — a stainless steel ribbon facade that seems to move as you pass. Car enthusiasts: this is mandatory. Everyone else: still worth 2 hours.

Exposition Park: The Olympic Hub Cluster

Exposition Park — immediately adjacent to the LA Memorial Coliseum and the Olympic swimming venue — contains two major museums. If you're attending athletics or swimming events, these are steps away from your venue and make an excellent morning activity before an afternoon event.

California Science Center

📍 Exposition Park (Olympic Zone) 🚇 Metro E Line: Expo/Vermont or Expo/Western 💰 General exhibits free / IMAX & Endeavour extra
Space Shuttle Endeavour

The centerpiece: Space Shuttle Endeavour, the orbiter that flew 25 missions between 1992 and 2011, now on permanent display. Seeing a full-size Space Shuttle in person is one of those experiences that's genuinely more powerful than anticipated — the scale of the vehicle, the understanding of what it survived (temperatures from -250°F in space to 3,000°F on re-entry), and the context of what it represents in human achievement. The Endeavour Experience gallery includes the external tank and solid rocket boosters in a reconstructed launch configuration. Opening 2026-2027 in the new Samuel Oschin Air and Space Center.

Other Exhibits

World of Life (the biology of living systems), Ecosystems (ocean, desert, rainforest, urban environments), Creative World (technology and design). The entire general collection is free. The IMAX theatre costs extra. This is the best science museum in Southern California and a particularly good choice if you're visiting with children.

Natural History Museum of Los Angeles

📍 Exposition Park (Olympic Zone) 🚇 Metro E Line: Expo/Vermont 💰 $15 adults / under 3 free

One of the 10 largest natural history museums in the world, with 35 million specimens. The dinosaur hall — three T. rex specimens including a growth series showing juvenile to adult — is the most impressive in any American museum outside the Smithsonian. African and North American mammal dioramas that have been in continuous use since 1913. Gems and minerals collection. Pre-Columbian California and the broader American West. The Citizen Science garden outside has live insect and butterfly specimens native to California. Directly adjacent to the Olympic swim venue — morning visit before afternoon events is an excellent structure for your day.

Contemporary Art

The Broad

📍 Grand Avenue / DTLA 🚇 Metro B/D Line: Pershing Square 💰 Free (timed entry reservation required)

Eli and Edythe Broad's contemporary art museum on Grand Avenue in DTLA holds a 2,000-work collection of post-1958 art: Jeff Koons, Cindy Sherman, Jasper Johns, Ed Ruscha, Roy Lichtenstein, Jean-Michel Basquiat, and contemporary artists working now. Admission is free but timed entry reservations are required — book at thebroad.org in advance. The building itself, designed by Diller Scofidio + Renfro with a "veil and vault" concept, is a significant piece of architecture. The Jeff Koons Balloon Dog sculpture (orange) visible through the ground floor windows can be seen without a ticket.

Hammer Museum

📍 Westwood / UCLA 🚌 Metro Rapid 720 or 2 bus 💰 Free always

The Hammer is UCLA's free contemporary and modern art museum and one of LA's most interesting art spaces. Consistently strong programming in contemporary art, film, and public discourse. The permanent collection includes Honoré Daumier works and continues expanding with contemporary acquisitions. Free public programming runs throughout the year. A more approachable scale than LACMA for visitors who find large museums overwhelming.

Museum Day Strategy for Olympics Visitors

Build museum visits around your event schedule. Morning events at Exposition Park: visit the Natural History Museum or California Science Center the day before. Afternoon events at DTLA venues: morning visit to The Broad or Grand Central Market first. Free days between events: dedicate a full day to LACMA + La Brea Tar Pits or the Getty Center. Most LA museums are closed Monday, so plan your free days accordingly. LACMA, the Getty, and the Natural History Museum all have excellent restaurants or cafes where you can eat without leaving the property.

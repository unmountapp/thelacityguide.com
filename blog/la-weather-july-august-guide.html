LA Weather in July & August: What to Expect at the Olympics

Coastal fog, inland heat, UV levels, and exactly what to pack for outdoor events.

The 2028 Olympics run from July 14 to July 30. This is simultaneously the best and most challenging time of year to be in Los Angeles. The weather is almost never rainy — LA gets less than 1 inch of rain total in July and August combined. But "good weather" in LA in summer means very different things depending on which part of the city you're in. The coast can be cool and foggy at 9am while Pasadena is hitting 95°F by noon. Here's what you actually need to know.

The Two LA Summers

Los Angeles isn't one climate — it's a gradient. The Pacific Ocean keeps the coast mild and sometimes cool; as you move inland, temperatures rise sharply. The Santa Ana Mountains trap heat in the valleys. Understanding this geography prevents the most common visitor mistake: packing only for beach weather and freezing at an indoor arena at night, or packing only for heat and dying at an outdoor stadium in the afternoon.

Coastal Zone — Santa Monica, Venice, Long Beach

72°FAverage Day
60°FAverage Night
85°FHot Days

The Pacific Ocean creates a marine layer — a low fog — that often sits along the coast through the morning. This is called "June Gloom" though it continues into July. Most days the fog burns off by 11am–noon and gives way to clear, sunny afternoons. The air is noticeably cooler here than inland, and evenings require a light layer. At the Long Beach sailing venue, wind is a constant factor — temperatures feel lower than they are.

Central LA — DTLA, Exposition Park, Koreatown, Inglewood

85°FAverage Day
67°FAverage Night
100°FHeat Waves

The Olympic core: Exposition Park (Coliseum, swim stadium, BMX track), SoFi Stadium in Inglewood, and the DTLA venues. Temperatures here are significantly warmer than the coast. The LA Memorial Coliseum is an open-air stadium — afternoon events can be genuinely hot. The swim venue has some shade structures but is substantially exposed. Heat waves — 4–7 day stretches of 95–105°F — occur 2–3 times in a typical July. They are forecast well in advance.

Inland Zone — Pasadena (Rose Bowl), San Fernando Valley

95°FAverage Day
70°FAverage Night
108°FHeat Waves

The Rose Bowl in Pasadena is the hottest Olympic venue. Pasadena sits in a valley ringed by mountains that trap heat. July average highs here are 96°F, and heat waves push it past 105°F. Afternoon football (soccer) games at the Rose Bowl in July will be physically demanding for spectators. The venue has shade in some areas but the open bowl design means direct sun on much of the seating. Plan for this.

⚠️ Outdoor Venue Heat Safety

At outdoor venues in July, especially the Coliseum and Rose Bowl, heat exhaustion is a genuine risk. LA28 will provide water stations and shaded rest areas. From your end: wear sun-protective clothing (UPF 50+ is sold at sports stores), apply SPF 50 sunscreen every 2 hours, drink water before you're thirsty, identify the nearest medical tent when you arrive. Children and elderly attendees are particularly vulnerable. This is not alarmism — LA summer sun at sea level is genuinely intense.

UV Index: Higher Than You Think

Los Angeles sits at 34°N latitude. In July, the UV index regularly reaches 10–11+ (Very High to Extreme) between 11am and 3pm. This is UV radiation equivalent to tropical beach conditions. Sunburn can occur in 15–20 minutes of unprotected exposure during these hours. Many visitors from northern Europe or East Asia underestimate this. A hat with a brim, UV-blocking clothing, and SPF 50 reapplied regularly are not optional considerations for all-day outdoor event attendance.

What About Rain?

July and August are the driest months of the year in Los Angeles. Average rainfall in July is 0.03 inches — essentially zero. It can rain; it almost never does. You do not need to pack a rain jacket for outdoor events. What you occasionally get instead is a light coastal drizzle in the very early morning that burns off before 10am. This will not affect any outdoor events.

Morning Fog at Coastal Venues

If you're attending early morning events at Long Beach or the cycling venues near the coast, the marine layer can create overcast, slightly cool conditions that feel jarring if you expected beach weather. A light layer for the morning warm-up isn't a bad idea even in July. By mid-morning it's usually cleared.

Evening Events: Pleasantly Surprising

One of the best-kept LA weather secrets: summer evenings are genuinely comfortable. By 7–8pm, temperatures drop 15–20°F from the afternoon peak. A 95°F afternoon at the Rose Bowl becomes a 75°F evening. This makes the 7pm and 8pm evening sessions at outdoor venues among the most pleasant viewing conditions. Track and field finals, swimming finals, and opening/closing ceremonies are scheduled for evening partly because of this — the athletes perform better and the spectators are more comfortable. If you're scheduling between events, choose evening tickets for outdoor venues over afternoon whenever possible.

Venue-by-Venue Weather Guide

LA Memorial Coliseum — Athletics, Opening/Closing Ceremonies

88°FAfternoon
73°FEvening

Open-air venue in Exposition Park, south-facing bowl. Afternoon sessions (1–5pm) will be hot; evening sessions (7pm onward) are comfortable. The track events and field finals are scheduled for evening. The Opening and Closing Ceremonies start at dusk. Bring water and sun protection for any daytime session; a light layer for late evening sessions when the temperature drops.

Rose Bowl — Football (Soccer)

96°FAfternoon
76°FEvening

The hottest Olympic venue. Open bowl, Pasadena valley location, full afternoon sun on much of the seating. Football group stage games are often scheduled during the day — come extremely prepared if you have afternoon tickets. Plenty of water, wide-brim hat, sunscreen. Evening matches here are much more comfortable and often beautiful as the mountains turn gold at sunset.

SoFi Stadium / Intuit Dome — Gymnastics, Basketball, Boxing

72°FIndoor

Both SoFi and Intuit Dome are air-conditioned indoor venues. Temperature inside is regulated regardless of outdoor conditions. The relief from summer heat makes these venues feel particularly comfortable after an outdoor session earlier in the day. Bring a light layer — arena air conditioning can feel cold during long sessions.

What to Pack for Olympic Events

Outdoor Day Events

  • Wide-brim hat (not a cap — you need brim on all sides)
  • UPF 50+ long-sleeve shirt
  • SPF 50 sunscreen (reapply hourly)
  • Refillable water bottle (2L minimum)
  • Electrolyte packets or sports drink
  • Light breathable shorts or linen trousers
  • Comfortable walking shoes (you walk more than you expect)

Outdoor Evening Events

  • Light layer jacket (temperatures drop)
  • Comfortable shoes
  • Water bottle
  • Small day bag for gear
  • Phone charger bank (long days drain batteries)

Indoor Venues

  • Light layer (AC can be cold)
  • Comfortable shoes
  • Small bag (venue security scans everything)

Beach / Exploring Days

  • Swimwear and beach towel
  • Flip flops or sandals
  • SPF 50 water-resistant sunscreen
  • Sunglasses (polarised for water glare)
  • Light cardigan for evening coastal chill

Air Quality

LA's air quality has improved dramatically since the 1970s but summer can still bring days with elevated ozone or particulate matter, particularly during heat waves when smog forms more easily. The AQI (Air Quality Index) in LA averages "Good" to "Moderate" most July and August days. Occasionally it reaches "Unhealthy for Sensitive Groups." If you have asthma or respiratory conditions, check AirNow.gov before outdoor events. An N95 mask provides significant protection if you need it — these are widely available at LA pharmacies.

Climate Smart Scheduling

If you're choosing between morning and afternoon session tickets for outdoor venues, choose morning. The hours between 7am–11am are the coolest, least humid, and have the lowest UV. This is when you want to be at the Rose Bowl or Coliseum. Afternoons from noon–4pm are peak heat; afternoons from 6pm onward are ideal. Indoor events work at any time.

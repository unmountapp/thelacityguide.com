Best Things To Do in LA for First-Time Visitors

What's genuinely worth your time — and what you can skip.

Los Angeles is the only city where you can eat tacos on a beach, hike above the clouds, browse billion-dollar art collections for free, and watch a film being shot on a street corner — all before dinner. But it's also genuinely enormous. Without a plan, you'll spend your trip sitting in traffic between things that don't fit together. This guide cuts through the noise and tells you what's actually worth going out of your way for.

The Genuine Bucket List

Worth it

The Getty Center

📍 Brentwood (West LA) 🚌 Metro Rapid 761 from Westwood 💰 Free admission

The Getty is one of the great surprises of LA — a world-class museum that charges nothing and sits on a hilltop with jaw-dropping views of the city, the ocean, and on a clear day, Catalina Island. The architecture alone by Richard Meier is worth the trip. The collection covers van Gogh, Rembrandt, Monet, and ancient manuscripts. Go on a Tuesday morning before crowds arrive. Trams run from the base parking structure every few minutes. This is a full half-day minimum.

Worth it

Griffith Observatory

📍 Los Feliz 🚌 DASH Observatory bus from Vermont/Sunset Metro 💰 Free (planetarium shows cost extra)

You've seen this building in a hundred movies. In person it's even better. Griffith Observatory sits on the south slope of Mount Hollywood with arguably the best view of the LA basin anywhere. On clear days you see the downtown skyline, the Hollywood Hills, and the Pacific. The hike up from the Greek Theatre parking lot takes about 30 minutes and delivers the best views of the Hollywood Sign you'll find without a tour. Go at sunset and stay for the star viewing through the telescopes — it's free after 7pm on clear nights.

Worth it

Grand Central Market

📍 Downtown LA (DTLA) 🚇 Pershing Square Metro B/D Line 💰 Stalls from $5

Running since 1917, Grand Central Market is a 35,000-square-foot food hall on the ground floor of a historic building at Broadway and 3rd. This is the real LA food scene compressed into one space: Eggslut for breakfast sandwiches, Ramen Hood for vegan ramen, Wexler's Deli for pastrami, DTLA Cheese for wine and boards, Ana Maria for Oaxacan tlayudas. The crowd is a genuine cross-section of the city. Come hungry, come at lunch on a weekday when lines are shorter, and plan to eat at two or three stalls.

Worth it

Venice Beach Boardwalk

📍 Venice 🚌 Big Blue Bus Rapid 3 from Santa Monica 💰 Free

Venice Boardwalk is one of those LA experiences that's simultaneously chaotic, confusing, and completely unmissable. Street performers, fortune tellers, a permanent outdoor weight pit that's been there since 1954, murals everywhere, skate parks, and a canal neighborhood two blocks away that feels like Amsterdam. Walk the boardwalk from Rose Avenue south to the canals, rent a bike from one of the stalls, and grab lunch at Gjusta bakery or a fish taco from the trucks on the beach side. Go on a Sunday when the crowds give it proper energy.

Worth it

LACMA (LA County Museum of Art)

📍 Mid-Wilshire / Museum Row 🚇 Wilshire/La Brea Metro E Line 💰 $25 adults / under 18 free

The largest art museum west of Chicago, LACMA's collection spans 6,000 years of world art. The permanent collection is genuinely extraordinary — one of the best Islamic art holdings in the world, a strong Asian art department, and a contemporary wing that includes Basquiat, Koons, and Hockney. The outdoor Urban Light installation (those 202 antique streetlamps) is free to visit anytime and one of the most photographed spots in LA. Combined with the Page Museum and La Brea Tar Pits next door, this is a full day on Museum Row.

Hollywood: Honest Advice

Hollywood gets complicated. The idea of Hollywood — the myth, the glamour, the movies — is magnificent. The actual blocks around Hollywood and Highland on a Saturday afternoon are... less so. Here's how to sort the genuine from the gimmick.

Worth it

Hollywood Walk of Fame

📍 Hollywood Blvd between La Brea and Vine 🚇 Hollywood/Highland Metro B Line

Walk it once. See Marilyn Monroe's star, find your favourite film or musician, take the photo. Budget 30–45 minutes. The stars start at La Brea Avenue and run east to Vine Street — the densest concentration of notable names is between Highland and Cahuenga. Walk the north side of the boulevard east, cross, and walk back west. Done. Don't pay anyone for photos in costumes; don't buy star maps from the street vendors.

Worth it

TCL Chinese Theatre

📍 6925 Hollywood Blvd

The forecourt — where you find the celebrity handprints and footprints in cement — is free to access. This is the genuine article. Marilyn Monroe's signature, Muhammad Ali's glove prints, Harry Potter cast footprints, Spider-Man's web imprint. Give it 20 minutes and you'll have seen everything. Skip the paid theatre tours unless you're a serious cinema nerd.

Depends on you

Warner Bros. Studio Tour

📍 Burbank (north of Hollywood) 💰 From $75/person

If you're a serious film or TV person, this is the best studio tour in LA. You ride through active production lots, see original sets and props, and the Harry Potter section is excellent. The tour takes 3 hours. Worth the price if movies matter to you; skip it if you just want to say you went to Hollywood.

Skip

Hollywood Wax Museum / Madame Tussauds

Expensive, crowded, and the wax figures look nothing like the celebrities. Spend the money on a meal instead.

The Hollywood Sign

You cannot walk up to the Hollywood Sign. The fence around it is alarmed and monitored. What you're looking for is a great view of it — and there are several, with very different tradeoffs.

Best Hollywood Sign Views

Griffith Observatory (free, easy): 15-minute walk from the observatory parking area. Clear view across the hills. Lake Hollywood Park (free, easy): Drive up to the small park on Lake Hollywood Drive — the sign appears directly above the reservoir, incredible for photos. Mulholland Drive pullouts (free, driving): Several pullouts along Mulholland offer panoramic city views with the sign visible. The Wisdom Tree Hike (free, moderate): 30-minute uphill hike from Cahuenga Peak trailhead puts you directly adjacent to the sign — closest you'll legally get.

Day Structure Tips

LA is not a walking city and distances that look manageable on a map take 45 minutes by car with traffic. Build your days around geography, not interest category. One day: West Side (Getty + Venice + Santa Monica). One day: DTLA (Grand Central Market + LACMA + Museum Row). One day: Hollywood + Griffith area. This prevents you from driving from Malibu to Downtown twice in the same day, which is a mistake everyone makes once.

Getting Around

The Metro E Line (Expo Line) connects DTLA to Santa Monica in 47 minutes with no traffic. It stops at USC/Exposition Park (Olympic venues), Culver City, and Santa Monica. For most visitors during the Olympics, this is your primary line. Uber/Lyft surge badly around events — build in 2× normal travel time after any major venue activity.

Food: The Real LA Experience

The best meals in LA are almost never in the tourist zones. The city's extraordinary diversity means the authentic flavours are in the neighbourhoods: Armenian in Glendale, Japanese in Sawtelle, Ethiopian in Little Ethiopia on Fairfax, Korean anywhere in Koreatown, Vietnamese in the San Gabriel Valley, Oaxacan in Boyle Heights. Going 20 minutes out of your way for lunch is usually worth it. Grand Central Market is the exception — genuine quality in an accessible location.

For Olympics Visitors Specifically

If you're in LA for the 2028 Olympics, your venue schedule will anchor your days. But LA has enough to fill every non-event hour for two weeks. The Exposition Park cluster (Coliseum, swim stadium, BMX) puts you walking distance from the California Science Center and the Natural History Museum — both excellent. The Inglewood cluster (SoFi, Intuit Dome) is a short drive from Manhattan Beach and Hermosa Beach, which are the most relaxed beach towns in the LA area. Long Beach has the Aquarium of the Pacific and a walkable waterfront that most visitors skip entirely.

Plan Your First Day Right

Arrive, check in, then go immediately to Grand Central Market for lunch regardless of jet lag. Walk the area — the Bradbury Building next door has the most beautiful interior atrium in the city and is free to enter during business hours. Take the Metro E Line west to Santa Monica for sunset on the pier. That's a perfect first afternoon in LA: DTLA, the best market in the city, the pier at golden hour. Cost: under $25 per person including food.

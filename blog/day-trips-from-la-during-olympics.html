Best Day Trips From LA During the 2028 Olympics

When you need to escape the crowds and recharge before the next event.

LA28 runs for 17 days. Nobody watches 17 days of continuous Olympic sport — there will be rest days, travel days between events, and mornings when you just need to be somewhere quieter. Southern California is extraordinarily well positioned for escapes: a wine country small town 90 minutes north, a Mexican border city 2 hours south, a desert resort town 2 hours east, and a bizarre alien landscape only 3 hours away. All of them are better experienced with a car, though the train serves Santa Barbara and San Diego admirably.

Olympics Traffic Warning

During July–August 2028, freeway traffic around LA will be significantly heavier than normal. Add 30–45 minutes to any driving estimate you find on Google Maps for trips departing before 10am or arriving back after 4pm. Train travel eliminates this entirely for Santa Barbara and San Diego.

Santa Barbara — The Easiest Escape

Santa Barbara

🚗 90 min drive (US-101 N) 🚆 2.5 hrs by Amtrak Pacific Surfliner 💰 Day trip cost: $60–120/person
Why Go

Santa Barbara is the most immediately beautiful small city in California: Spanish Colonial architecture painted white with red-tile roofs, set against the Santa Ynez mountains that fall directly into the Pacific. State Street runs from the beach to the hills and has every restaurant and shop you need. The Santa Barbara Mission (1786) is genuinely impressive. The waterfront Stearns Wharf dates to 1872 and has good seafood restaurants.

What to Do

Walk the beach east to the harbor in the morning — it's quiet and the light is extraordinary. Have a late breakfast at Scarlett Begonia (sustainable California cuisine). Walk State Street north to the mission. Lunch at La Super Rica Taqueria — Julia Child's favourite Mexican restaurant anywhere, still run by the same family, still cash only, still worth the wait. Catch the Amtrak back before 5pm to avoid the evening rush. Perfect one-day structure.

Wine Country Extension

If you have a car and want to add a night, the Santa Ynez Valley (Sideways country) is 40 minutes inland. Los Olivos is a small wine village with tasting rooms in walkable distance. Book accommodation in Solvang — the Dutch-themed town is kitschy but charming, with genuine Danish pastry bakeries and inexpensive hotels.

Train Option

The Amtrak Pacific Surfliner from LA Union Station to Santa Barbara runs along the coast — some of the most scenic rail travel in the US. Book on amtrak.com. The train takes 2.5 hours, costs around $35–45 each way, and deposits you near the waterfront. You can walk to everything in Santa Barbara without a car.

San Diego — Beach + Culture + Food

San Diego

🚗 2 hrs drive (I-5 S) 🚆 2.5–3 hrs Amtrak Pacific Surfliner 💰 Day trip: $80–150/person
Why Go

San Diego is a proper city — 1.4 million people — with a distinct identity from LA. The neighborhoods are walkable in a way LA is not. The weather is reliably perfect in July (low 70s, no marine layer). The food scene is excellent, particularly for Mexican food (you're 20 minutes from Tijuana) and seafood. Balboa Park contains 17 museums and the best zoo in North America.

What to Do

Take the Amtrak to Santa Fe Depot in downtown San Diego. Walk through the Gaslamp Quarter to Balboa Park. The San Diego Museum of Art and the Natural History Museum are both excellent; the Fleet Science Center is good if you have kids. Take the bus or a rideshare to La Jolla Cove in the afternoon — sea lions sunbathing on the rocks below clifftop restaurants. If you have one dinner in San Diego, eat it in Little Italy at Bracero Cocina de Raiz (upscale Mexican) or Underbelly (ramen with San Diego craft beer).

Consider Overnight

San Diego deserves more than one day. The Hotel del Coronado on Coronado Island is a Victorian beachfront hotel (opened 1888) that's one of the great American hotel experiences. A night there with the beach, the sunset, and dinner in the Crown Room restaurant is a genuine splurge worth making.

Palm Springs — The Desert Oasis

Palm Springs

🚗 2 hrs drive (I-10 E) ⚠️ No train service 💰 Day trip: $80–160/person
Why Go

Palm Springs is a mid-century modernist time capsule in the Sonoran Desert. The Rat Pack drank here. Frank Sinatra had a house here. The entire town is filled with perfectly preserved 1950s and 60s architecture. The Aerial Tramway climbs 8,516 feet in 10 minutes and deposits you in a pine forest above the desert floor where temperatures are 30–40°F cooler than below.

Warning: July Heat

Palm Springs in July averages 108°F (42°C) at ground level. This is extreme heat. Plan your day around it: arrive early, do outdoor activities before 11am, retreat to a hotel pool or air-conditioned museum from noon to 4pm, re-emerge for sunset. The tramway summit is legitimately cool and comfortable all day. Do not attempt hiking on the desert floor after 10am in July.

What to Do

Leave LA by 7am. Drive the 10 Freeway east through the Inland Empire. Stop at Cabazon for the famous dinosaur statues (a very LA/California roadside attraction). In Palm Springs: take the Aerial Tramway first thing. Lunch at Cheeky's (brunch institution, great bloody mary). Walk Palm Canyon Drive to look at the architecture. Swim. Have dinner at Workshop Kitchen + Bar or Wilma & Frieda before driving back in the cooler evening.

Joshua Tree — The Otherworldly Option

Joshua Tree National Park

🚗 2.5–3 hrs drive (I-10 E + CA-62 N) ⚠️ No train service 💰 Day trip: $60–100/person + $35 park fee
Why Go

Joshua Tree National Park is one of the strangest landscapes on Earth. Ancient boulder formations the size of buildings sit in orange sand. The namesake Joshua trees (actually yuccas) grow in formations that look like illustrations from Dr. Seuss. The park sits at the junction of two desert ecosystems — the Mojave and the Sonoran — and the boundary between them is visible as you drive through. At night, with no light pollution, the stars are unlike anything you'll see in the city.

Summer Caveats

July temperatures in Joshua Tree reach 110–115°F (43–46°C). This is not an exaggeration. People die from heat exposure in this park every summer. Visit for sunrise only (leave LA at 4am, arrive by 6:30am), do 2–3 hours in the park before 10am, and leave before noon. Alternatively, stay for sunset and the night sky. The park is spectacular from dusk through dawn — plan around the heat, not against it.

What to Do

Cholla Cactus Garden (easy, 0.25-mile loop). Skull Rock Trail (1.7 miles, interesting rock formations). Cap Rock (easy, flat, beautiful for sunrise). Hidden Valley (1 mile loop, dramatic boulders). Drive through Pinto Basin for the other-worldly scale. Stop in the town of Joshua Tree (not the park) at Crossroads Cafe for breakfast. Camp if you want the night sky — book sites at recreation.gov months ahead for any summer weekend.

Catalina Island — The Underrated One

Catalina Island

🚢 1 hr ferry from San Pedro or Long Beach 💰 $80–100/person round trip ferry
Why Go

Twenty-two miles off the LA coast sits a Mediterranean-style island that most visitors to LA never visit. The small town of Avalon has no cars (golf carts only), clear turquoise water with excellent snorkeling, paddleboarding, and kayaking, and a peaceful pace that Los Angeles itself cannot offer. This is the best beach day in Southern California if you're willing to take the ferry.

What to Do

Take the Catalina Express ferry from San Pedro (near the Olympic sailing venue) or Long Beach. The crossing takes 60–75 minutes and is usually calm. In Avalon: snorkel Casino Point Underwater Park (entry fee, excellent visibility, orange garibaldi fish are everywhere). Rent a kayak and paddle the coastline. Lunch at Avalon Grille on the harbor. Take the ferry back by 4pm to avoid the evening rush. The entire day costs around $120–150 per person including ferry, snorkel rental, and lunch — excellent value for an experience this good.

Olympics Timing Note

The Long Beach venue cluster hosts sailing events during the Olympics. If you time a Catalina day trip around a sailing event day, you can take the Catalina Express from Long Beach before or after watching races — the ferry terminal is walking distance from the Olympic sailing venue. This makes Catalina the only day trip on this list that integrates directly with the Olympic schedule.

Planning Your Escape Days

The 2028 Olympics schedule has natural rest days built in — Opening and Closing Ceremonies bookend the event, and several sports have qualification rounds followed by a day gap before finals. Look at your event tickets and identify which days you have nothing scheduled. Those are your escape days. Book accommodation (if staying overnight) at least 6–8 months ahead for July–August 2028 — Southern California's entire tourism infrastructure will be under pressure during the Games.

Safest Areas to Stay in Los Angeles for LA 2028 Families

Published: August 2026 · 7 min read · The LA City Guide Editorial Team

"Safe" in a city as large as LA is relative to block and time of day more than neighborhood as a whole. For families visiting during the 2028 Olympics, the more useful question is: which areas are calm, walkable, and easy to navigate with kids in tow?

What Makes a Neighborhood Family-Friendly Here

Look for well-lit streets, nearby grocery options, manageable walking distances to transit, and a slower nighttime pace. LA neighborhoods vary block to block, so these are broad patterns rather than guarantees.

Santa Monica

Why it works: Flat, walkable streets, direct Metro access, and family-oriented dining near the Pier and Third Street Promenade.

Pasadena

Why it works: Quieter than central LA, close to the Rose Bowl, with Old Town Pasadena offering an easy, contained area to explore on foot.

Pasadena is a strong pick if your family's schedule centers on Rose Bowl events, avoiding late-night travel back from Downtown.

Parts of the San Fernando Valley

Why it works: More suburban pace, often better value on larger accommodations for bigger families, though it requires more driving or longer Metro/bus connections.

Hotel vs. Apartment vs. House Rental

Hotels offer more predictability and staff on-site. Apartment or house rentals give families more space and kitchen access, useful for keeping food costs down over a longer trip — see our LA Olympics budget guide for detailed cost comparisons.

Checking Safety Around Your Accommodation

Look at recent local reviews, check walking distance to the nearest Metro or bus stop, and confirm rideshare availability in the immediate area before booking.

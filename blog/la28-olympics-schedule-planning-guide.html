LA28 Olympics Schedule Planning Guide

Turn the official competition schedule into a realistic itinerary with zones, buffers and backup plans.

September 30, 2026 · 10 min read · The LA City Guide Editorial Team

The official schedule tells you when competition happens; it does not tell you whether your personal itinerary is realistic. This guide shows how to convert tickets and wish-list sessions into a Los Angeles plan that protects travel time and energy.

Schedule status: LA28 competition dates, times and venues can change. Use the official schedule for decisions and treat third-party calendars as planning aids only.

Start With the Fixed Dates

The Olympic Opening Ceremony is scheduled for July 14, 2028, and the Closing Ceremony for July 30. The published competition schedule covers a large number of sports, sessions and venue zones. Some competition activity may begin before the Opening Ceremony, so check your sport rather than using the ceremony as your arrival-date shortcut.

Download the latest schedule from the official LA28 Games Plan. Record the version date because venue and session updates may occur between planning and ticket delivery.

Build a Session Spreadsheet

Create one row per session and include more than the start time. A useful plan needs the venue zone, estimated entry time, transit route, exit buffer and ticket holder.

Think in Zones, Not Miles

Two venues can look close on a regional map and still require transfers, controlled walking routes and long post-event queues. Group consecutive sessions in the same zone whenever possible: Downtown and Exposition Park; Long Beach waterfront venues; Inglewood venues; or Pasadena-area events.

Avoid pairing distant zones unless the gap is generous and the second ticket is not emotionally or financially irreplaceable. Our venue-zones guide explains the geography in plain language.

Use the Two-Event Rule Carefully

Two sessions in one day can work when they share a zone, are separated by several hours and the first event has a predictable finish. Three ticketed sessions usually leave little resilience for overtime, transit disruption, meals and screening.

Do not use scheduled finish time as departure time. Add time to exit the seating area, move through the crowd, reach the station or shuttle queue and complete the trip.

Choose a Hotel From Your Schedule

Count nights and sessions by zone. The best hotel is usually near your most frequent cluster or beside a high-value transit connection—not necessarily near the most famous attraction. If your plan is split almost evenly between Long Beach and northern LA County, compare one hotel with a split stay.

Before paying, test the route at the same day and approximate hour using current transit tools. Games service will differ, but the exercise exposes long distances and weak late-night connections.

Protect Arrival and Departure Days

Do not place a must-see session shortly after a flight lands. Immigration, baggage, airport transfer and hotel check-in create too many dependencies. Likewise, avoid a late medal session before an early flight; ceremony and finals crowds can extend the return journey.

Use arrival day for orientation: collect essentials, test your transit card, walk the route to the nearest station and confirm mobile-ticket access.

Add Meals and Recovery

Schedule meals as deliberately as events. Venue food lines can be long, and neighborhoods may be busy after major sessions. Place an early meal near the venue and keep a portable option that complies with the day’s rules.

Build at least one lower-intensity half-day every three or four days. Heat, walking, crowds and late returns compound over a multi-day trip. A lighter day protects the sessions you value most.

Create a Live Update Routine

  1. One week before: verify every venue and session time.
  2. One day before: check ticket delivery, weather, transit and bag rules.
  3. Before departure: use the official journey planner and save the return route.
  4. After entry: identify the exit and transport point for the next move.
  5. After any change: update the shared itinerary for the whole group.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many Olympic sessions should I plan per day?

One or two is realistic for most visitors. Two works best in the same zone with a large gap; three creates significant schedule risk.

Should I book my hotel before building the schedule?

Use refundable options if you book early. Your confirmed venue pattern should drive the final hotel choice.

Where should I verify session changes?

Use LA28.org, your official ticket account and official Games communications. Recheck even if you previously downloaded a schedule.

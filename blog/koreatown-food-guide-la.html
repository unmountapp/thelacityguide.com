Koreatown Food Guide: LA's 24-Hour Dining Capital

The neighborhood that never closes, never disappoints, and never overcharges.

Koreatown — K-Town to everyone who lives here — is a 2.7-square-mile stretch of central LA that contains more restaurants per block than anywhere else in the city. It runs roughly between Vermont and Western avenues, north of the 10 freeway. At midnight on a Tuesday it has more foot traffic than most LA neighborhoods at noon on a Saturday. For Olympics visitors, it's the best base camp in the city: Metro accessible, central, affordable, and open when everything else has closed.

Why Koreatown for Olympics Visitors

K-Town sits at the intersection of two Metro lines — the B Line (Purple) and the D Line (Purple) run through Wilshire/Vermont and Wilshire/Western stations — making it one of the most transit-connected neighborhoods in LA. The Olympic venues at Exposition Park, DTLA, Inglewood, and Pasadena are all reachable without a car. Hotel prices here run 30–40% below comparable rooms in Santa Monica or Beverly Hills. And the food is extraordinary at every price point.

Getting Here

Metro B/D Line to Wilshire/Vermont (central K-Town) or Wilshire/Western (east side). From DTLA, it's 15 minutes. From the Expo Park venues, take the E Line east to 7th/Metro Center, transfer to the B Line west — about 25 minutes total. Parking is available in the Korea Town Galleria and Koreatown Plaza structures on weeknights if you're driving from Inglewood.

KBBQ: The Essential Experience

Korean BBQ is the centrepiece of any K-Town visit. You grill meat at your table — marinated short ribs (galbi), thinly sliced beef (bulgogi), pork belly (samgyupsal), or spicy pork (dwaeji bulgogi). The meat arrives with an array of banchan (small side dishes): kimchi, pickled radish, spinach, bean sprouts, fish cake, sweet black beans. A good BBQ meal for two with drinks runs $40–60 and takes 90 minutes minimum. Don't rush it.

Park's BBQ

KBBQ 9 pm – 2 am (late) $$$ · $50–70/person

Widely considered the best KBBQ in LA and one of the best in the country. Premium USDA prime cuts, expert tableside service, banchan that arrives in waves. The prime beef short rib is extraordinary. Waits are real — go at 5:30pm or after 9:30pm. Worth every penny of the premium over the competition.

Quarters Korean BBQ

KBBQ Open until 2 am $$ · $35–50/person

More casual than Park's, faster service, and a bit cheaper. The marinated cuts here are excellent, especially the bulgogi and the spicy pork. Good for groups — tables are large and the ventilation is strong. A reliable choice any night of the week without a long wait.

Soowon Galbi KBBQ

KBBQ Open until 3 am $$ · $30–45/person

The late-night option. Soowon stays open until 3am, making it the default destination after games or events. Specializes in galbi (short ribs) — the name literally means "short rib." No frills, no reservation system, walk-in only. The soybean paste stew (doenjang jjigae) served alongside is one of the best versions in the city.

Banchan Culture: Eating Like a Local

Banchan are the small complimentary dishes that arrive with every Korean meal. They're not appetizers — they're eaten alongside the main course, used to balance the richness of grilled meat or the heat of a stew. A good restaurant sends out 8–12 banchan. Refills are always free; just ask. The quality of a restaurant's banchan is the best indicator of its kitchen's care.

Common banchan you'll encounter: kimchi (fermented cabbage, the essential one), kongnamul (sesame-dressed bean sprouts), japchae (glass noodles with vegetables), gamja jorim (braised potatoes in a sweet soy glaze), eomuk bokkeum (stir-fried fish cake). Don't be shy about asking what something is — the staff at K-Town restaurants are used to first-timers and genuinely enjoy explaining.

Beyond BBQ: The Full K-Town Menu

Hanjip

Korean Fried Chicken Cocktail Bar $$ · $25–40/person

Korean fried chicken done properly — double-fried for maximum crunch, sauced in either soy garlic or sweet-spicy gochujang. Hanjip also has a serious cocktail program with soju-based drinks that are worth staying for. The crispy rice with assorted banchan appetizer is one of the best small plates in K-Town.

Sun Nong Dan

Galbi-jjim Stew $$ · $20–35/person

Famous for galbi-jjim — braised short ribs in a rich, slightly sweet sauce served on a bed of chewy rice cakes and glass noodles, arriving in a bubbling stone pot. This is the dish people drive across the city for. Order the cheese galbi-jjim at the end and let the melted cheese pull over everything. It sounds strange; it's magnificent.

Kobawoo House

Bossam Traditional Korean $$ · $25–40/person

A K-Town institution since 1983. The specialty is bossam — boiled pork belly sliced thin, served with fermented kimchi, raw oysters, and lettuce wraps. You assemble each bite yourself. The entire table shares from large platters. Dinner here feels communal in the best way. The soondubu jjigae (soft tofu stew with seafood) is also exceptional.

Where to Drink

Escala Bar

Cocktail Bar Open until 2 am

Korean-influenced cocktails in a dark, well-designed space. The soju-based drinks are lighter than you expect — lower ABV and more sessionable than Korean rice wine shots at the BBQ joints. Good for a pre-dinner drink or a post-BBQ wind-down.

The Normandie Club

Speakeasy Style Classic Cocktails

In the basement of The Line Hotel. Compact, dark, and genuinely excellent — one of the best craft cocktail bars in all of LA, not just K-Town. The bar team takes classic cocktail technique seriously. Get there before 9pm to avoid the wait.

Karaoke: The Third Act

K-Town karaoke is different from the Western version. You don't sing on a stage in front of strangers. You rent a private room (norebang) by the hour — typically $20–30/hour for the room, then you pay for drinks. The rooms come with large screens, tambourines, echo effects, and microphones. Your group, your songs, your dignity intact. It's genuinely one of the best late-night activities in the city.

Karaoke Rooms to Book

Pharaoh Karaoke Lounge — big rooms, good sound system, available until 2am. Coin Karaoke — newer, cleaner, popular with groups. Koreaoke — the most visible one with the best song selection including recent K-pop. Book ahead on weekends; walk-in is fine on weeknights.

The 24-Hour Option: Ham Ji Park

Ham Ji Park on 6th Street is open 24 hours. At 3am after a late event it is one of the only places in the city where you can sit down, order a full Korean meal, and eat properly. The pork shoulder (dwaeji galbi) is their specialty. It is crowded at 3am. It is always crowded at 3am. This is part of the experience.

K-Town as Olympics Base

During the 2028 Olympics, K-Town will be a natural gathering point for the Korean Olympic delegation and its enormous international fanbase. Korean athletes compete in archery, shooting, Taekwondo (invented in Korea), wrestling, and weightlifting — all disciplines where Korean teams traditionally medal. Expect the neighbourhood to have an electric atmosphere on days when Korean athletes compete. Find a BBQ restaurant with a TV, order the galbi, and watch the Games with people who are genuinely invested in them.

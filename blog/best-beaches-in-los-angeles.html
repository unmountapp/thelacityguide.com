Best Beaches in Los Angeles

27 miles of coastline. Here's where to spend your beach day.

The Los Angeles coastline runs from Malibu in the north to Long Beach in the south — 27 uninterrupted miles of Pacific beach. No single beach is best for everyone. Santa Monica has the most infrastructure and is most accessible by transit; Venice has the most character; Manhattan Beach and Hermosa are the most local; Zuma and Point Dume are the most scenic. Here's how to choose, and how to get there from your venue zone.

The Rankings

1

Santa Monica Beach Metro accessible

📍 Santa Monica 🚇 Metro E Line to Downtown Santa Monica 🚗 30 min from DTLA (no traffic) 🅿️ $15–25/day
The Experience

Santa Monica Beach is the most visitor-friendly beach in LA. The pier (built 1909) has rides, restaurants, and a functioning carousel. The Third Street Promenade, three blocks from the sand, has every chain restaurant and shop you might need. The bike path runs south along the beach all the way to Venice and Manhattan Beach. The water is calm enough for swimming, the lifeguard stations are numerous, and the crowds at peak summer are large but manageable.

Best For

First-time visitors. Families with children. Anyone arriving by public transit. People who want infrastructure around them (bathrooms, restaurants, showers). Evening sunsets over the pier are genuinely spectacular.

Skip It If

You want a quiet, uncrowded experience. Santa Monica is the most visited beach in LA and it shows on summer weekends. Arrive early (before 10am) or go on a weekday.

2

Venice Beach Metro accessible

📍 Venice 🚌 Big Blue Bus Rapid 3 from Santa Monica Metro 🚗 10 min from Santa Monica
The Experience

Venice has a character Santa Monica lacks. The boardwalk here is a genuine cultural institution — bodybuilders at Muscle Beach Outdoor Gym (in use since 1934), the skate park that produced Tony Hawk-era culture, street performers, murals everywhere, fortune tellers, and the strangest collection of vendors you'll encounter outside a festival. Two blocks inland, the Venice Canals are a residential neighborhood of pastel houses on actual gondola canals, built in 1905 to recreate Venice, Italy. It's now a very expensive residential neighborhood but the canals are public and free to walk.

Best For

Anyone who wants LA's authentic beach culture rather than a sanitised version. The people-watching alone is worth the trip. The beach itself is wide and the sand is good. Gjusta bakery on Abbot Kinney is one of the best breakfast spots in the city — go early, there's always a wait.

3

Manhattan Beach

📍 Manhattan Beach (South Bay) 🚌 LA Metro 130 bus from El Segundo Green Line 🚗 20 min from SoFi Stadium / Inglewood
The Experience

Manhattan Beach is where Angelenos who can afford it actually live near the beach. The pier is shorter than Santa Monica's but prettier. The downtown (Manhattan Beach Blvd running down to the pier) has excellent independent restaurants, coffee shops, and boutiques. The sand is wider and cleaner here than in Santa Monica. The water is clear and swimmable. Beach volleyball is huge here — the Manhattan Beach Open is a major tournament and the culture is embedded in the neighborhood.

Best For

Anyone staying in Inglewood or the South Bay for SoFi/Intuit Dome events. This is the easiest quality beach from the Inglewood venue cluster. Dinner at either Manhattan Beach Post (creative American) or Fishing With Dynamite (seafood) makes this a complete day out.

4

Hermosa Beach

📍 Hermosa Beach (South Bay) 🚌 LA Metro 130 bus 🚗 25 min from SoFi Stadium
The Experience

Immediately south of Manhattan Beach and more casual. Hermosa has a stronger bar and nightlife scene along the Pier Avenue strip, a wide flat beach, and the same excellent South Bay sand and water quality. The Strand — the pedestrian and cycling path along the beach — runs continuously between Hermosa and Manhattan Beach, making it easy to walk or bike between the two. This is the most relaxed of the South Bay beaches and the most social.

5

Zuma Beach & Point Dume

📍 Malibu (30 miles north) 🚌 LA Metro 534 bus from PCH 🚗 45–60 min from DTLA (no traffic)
The Experience

Zuma is the most purely beautiful beach in LA. Six miles of uninterrupted sand backed by the Santa Monica Mountains, with clearer water than anywhere in the South Bay. Point Dume, the headland at the north end, has dramatic cliffs, tidepools, and a view south along the coast that appears in approximately 40 movies and TV shows. Point Dume State Beach below the headland is smaller, rockier, and often has calmer water for swimming.

Best For

Anyone renting a car and wanting the most scenically spectacular beach day. The Pacific Coast Highway drive from Santa Monica to Zuma is one of the great coastal drives in America. Stop at Nobu Malibu for lunch if your budget allows (expensive but the view of the Pacific from the deck is unmatched). Alternatively, the Malibu Seafood Fresh Fish Market on PCH is a beloved local institution with excellent fish and chips at a fraction of the price.

Important Note

Malibu requires a car. The bus (Metro 534) is a very long journey from central LA. During Olympics traffic, the PCH can back up significantly between Santa Monica and Malibu — check conditions before committing to the drive.

From Your Venue Zone to the Beach

Exposition Park / DTLA Zone → Best Beach

Take the Metro E Line (Expo) west from Jefferson/USC or Expo/Vermont. Ride 47 minutes to Downtown Santa Monica. Walk 8 minutes to the beach. Total: under 1 hour, no car needed, $1.75 fare. This is the cleanest transit connection between any Olympic venue and a beach.

Inglewood / SoFi Zone → Best Beach

Rideshare or drive south on Prairie Avenue to Manhattan Beach or Hermosa Beach — about 20 minutes without traffic. Alternatively, the Metro Crenshaw/K Line connects from Inglewood to the E Line at Crenshaw/Exposition, then west to Santa Monica — but this takes 45–60 minutes. For the SoFi cluster, Manhattan Beach is the practical choice.

Pasadena / Rose Bowl Zone → Best Beach

No great transit option. Drive the 134 west to the 10 west, 35–50 minutes to Santa Monica without traffic. The 10 freeway connects Pasadena directly to the coast — this is the most scenic freeway approach to Santa Monica, arriving at the base of the pier. Budget 1.5 hours each way during Olympics traffic.

Water Temperature and Swimming

The Pacific Ocean off Los Angeles in July averages 65–68°F (18–20°C). This is cold by most international standards — significantly colder than the Mediterranean, Caribbean, or East Coast US in summer. You can swim comfortably, especially after 20 minutes of adjustment. Wetsuit rentals are available near most beaches if you want them. The water is warmest in late August. Rip currents are present year-round at exposed beaches like Zuma — always swim near a lifeguard tower, which are staffed daily from 9am to sunset at all LA County beaches.

Parking Tips

During the Olympics, beach parking will be worse than usual. At Santa Monica, the main beach lots fill by 10am on weekends — transit is strongly recommended. Manhattan Beach has street parking that requires a 2-hour limit; the parking structure on Valley Drive has a small fee and is usually available. Hermosa Beach parking on the Strand side is virtually impossible on summer weekends — walk or ride from Manhattan Beach. Zuma has a large paid lot (LA County Parks, $8–12) that is typically fine on weekdays and crowded on weekends.

LA is not like other Olympic cities. It has a proper Downtown — and it spans another 500 square miles of distinct neighborhoods beyond it. The traffic is genuinely challenging. The food, culture, and variety are genuinely exceptional.

This guide exists to help you navigate the real city — not a brochure version of it. We cover where to stay based on which venues you are attending, how to get between them without spending half your day in traffic, where to eat without getting taken for a tourist, and what to actually do between events.

We update this guide regularly as LA28 confirms new information. Everything time-sensitive has a date label. When we do not know something, we say so.

🚇 Getting Around LA During the 2028 Olympics

LA traffic is bad enough on a normal Tuesday. During the Games — with road closures, security perimeters, and hundreds of thousands of additional visitors daily — driving will be significantly harder. Plan your transport before you plan your events.

Last updated: June 2026 · Plan transit at metro.net · LA28 transport info at la28.org

⚠️ LA28 transport planning is ongoing. The official Games-time shuttle network, road closure zones, and security perimeters have not been fully confirmed as of June 2026. Information here reflects what Metro and LA28 have publicly announced. Check metro.net and la28.org for updates closer to July 2028.
🚇

Metro Rail

LA's expanding Metro connects most major Olympic venues. The B/D Lines serve Downtown, Koreatown, Hollywood, and the UCLA area. The E (Expo) Line connects Santa Monica to Downtown via Culver City and USC. The K Line serves Inglewood and SoFi Stadium. The A (Blue) Line connects to Long Beach.

→ Plan your route at metro.net
🚌

Olympic Shuttle Network

LA28 has committed to a venue-to-venue shuttle network for Games time. Specific routes, stops, and schedules have not been confirmed as of mid-2026. Check la28.org for official announcements when published. Do not plan event logistics around unconfirmed shuttle routes.

✈️

LAX Airport

The LAX Automated People Mover (AirTrain) opened in 2023 and connects all terminals to the Metro and car rental facilities. Budget 2.5 hours minimum for international arrivals. Terminals 7/8 and the Tom Bradley International Terminal (TBIT) handle the largest international flights.

→ LAX official info at flylax.com
🚗

Rideshare (Uber / Lyft)

Rideshare is widely available but surge pricing during major event days will be significant. Designated rideshare pickup zones will operate at each Olympic venue — follow venue signage. Book at least 30 minutes before you need to depart from a major event. Have a backup plan.

🚲

Metro Bike Share & Scooters

Metro Bike Share operates across LA with stations near many transit hubs. Particularly useful for the Venice–Santa Monica corridor. Bird and Lime scooters are available in beach areas. Helmet use is legally required in LA. Monthly passes available for Metro Bike.

→ Metro Bike info at bikeshare.metro.net
🚆

Regional Rail (Metrolink / Amtrak)

Metrolink connects LA to Anaheim (30 min for Disneyland day trips), with Amtrak Pacific Surfliner reaching San Diego (2.5–3 hrs) and Santa Barbara (2 hrs). Book early — trains sell out during peak Olympics weeks. Metrolink connects to the Metro system at Union Station Downtown.

→ Metrolink at metrolinktrains.com
🚗

Driving in LA During the Games

If you must drive: expect significant delays around all venue zones on event days. Road closures and security perimeters will affect major arterials including Figueroa, Sepulveda, and corridors around Inglewood. Parking at most venues will be extremely limited. Park-and-ride options with Metro connections are the most reliable approach.

♿

Accessibility

All Metro stations are ADA accessible with elevators. LA28 has committed to accessible venue design at all sites. Metro's Paratransit service (Access Services) is available across LA County. Contact Metro for accessibility trip planning.

→ Metro accessibility at metro.net

🚗 Day Trips from LA: What's Worth the Drive

LA puts you within reach of beaches, deserts, mountains, wine country, and a second major theme park within 3 hours. These are all realistic day trips — but drive times vary massively with traffic. Don't plan to attend a 7pm event in LA and leave Joshua Tree at 4pm.

Drive times are off-peak estimates. During the Games, I-5 and I-10 will be significantly busier. Add 30–60% to estimates during peak periods.

🏰 Disneyland Resort, Anaheim

Both Disneyland and Disney California Adventure parks, 40–90 minutes south depending on traffic. For summer 2028 you will need to book tickets months in advance — Disneyland regularly sells out on peak summer days. The Anaheim Regional Transportation Intermodal Center (ARTIC) has direct Amtrak and Metrolink connections from LA Union Station.

🕐 40–90 min drive 🚆 45 min by Metrolink from Union Station 💰 $100–180+ per person

🌊 Malibu & Pacific Coast Highway

Drive PCH north from Santa Monica and stop at Zuma Beach, Point Dume, and El Matador State Beach. The drive itself along the cliffs is the attraction. Lunch at Nobu Malibu or Geoffrey's. Return via Mulholland Drive at sunset for views over the Valley. No public transport — you need a car or rideshare.

🕐 35–60 min from Santa Monica 🚗 Car or rideshare only 💰 Low cost unless you eat at Nobu

🍷 Santa Barbara & Wine Country

Santa Barbara itself is a beautiful Spanish Colonial city with a good beach, great restaurants, and a walkable State Street. The Santa Ynez Valley wine country (the Sideways territory) is another 45 minutes north — wineries, Solvang (a Danish village that is exactly what it sounds like), and 40+ tasting rooms. Amtrak Pacific Surfliner from Union Station runs directly to Santa Barbara.

🕐 1.5–2 hrs drive 🚆 2 hrs by Amtrak Pacific Surfliner 💰 Variable — wine tasting adds up

🏜️ Joshua Tree National Park

Surreal boulder landscapes, excellent rock climbing, and the best stargazing in Southern California. The park is genuinely striking at dawn and dusk. Combine with Palm Springs for a comfortable overnight — Palm Springs has good hotels, the tram up to San Jacinto, and excellent midcentury architecture. Not practical as a same-day return if you have an evening event in LA.

🕐 2.5 hrs drive from LA 🚗 Car required — no public transport to the park 💰 $35 vehicle entry fee (America the Beautiful annual pass $80 covers this and all national parks)

🐬 San Diego

Balboa Park, the Zoo, Little Italy, La Jolla Cove, and Coronado Island. World-class beaches. Also hosting some Olympic sailing events in 2028. Worth an overnight if you can manage it. Amtrak Pacific Surfliner runs from Union Station to San Diego Santa Fe Depot — roughly 3 hours, scenic coastal route.

🕐 2–3 hrs drive (I-5 South) 🚆 ~3 hrs by Amtrak Pacific Surfliner 💰 Variable — zoo entry ~$65 adult

🌲 Big Bear Lake

Mountain escape 2 hours east. Hiking, paddleboarding, and genuine alpine air at 6,752 feet elevation — a sharp contrast to July heat in LA. The winding highway up to the lake (CA-18 or CA-38) is part of the experience. No direct public transport from LA — car required.

🕐 2–2.5 hrs drive 🚗 Car required 💰 Low cost — mainly accommodation if overnight

🌍 International Visitors: What You Actually Need to Know

Los Angeles is one of the most internationally diverse cities on earth — but arriving without preparation is still a mistake. Visa rules, health costs, tipping culture, and transport all have real implications for your trip.

Last updated: June 2026 · Always verify current entry requirements at travel.state.gov and your country's foreign ministry before booking.

⚠️ Visa and entry requirements change. The information below reflects rules as of June 2026. Always verify at esta.cbp.dhs.gov (for ESTA) and travel.state.gov before booking your trip.

🇬🇧 UK Visitors

  • Entry: ESTA required (not a visa, but must be applied for online at esta.cbp.dhs.gov — $21 fee, apply at least 72 hours before travel)
  • Time zone: LA is 8 hours behind London (BST)
  • Drive side: Drive on the right
  • Tipping: 18–20% at restaurants — not optional
  • LA community: Large British expat presence in West Hollywood and Santa Monica

🇯🇵 Japanese Visitors

  • Entry: ESTA required (same as UK — apply online in advance)
  • Time zone: LA is 16–17 hours behind Tokyo
  • Language: Little Tokyo in Downtown LA has Japanese restaurants, supermarkets, and Japanese-speaking staff
  • Cash: LA is more card-friendly than Japan — but carry some USD for street food and trucks
  • Transport: Rideshare apps work well; punctuality differs from Japanese rail

🇦🇺 Australian Visitors

  • Entry: ESTA required — apply online before travel
  • Time zone: LA is 17–18 hours behind Sydney (AEST)
  • Drive side: Drive on the right
  • Coffee: Flat whites are now widely available in LA — Venice and Silver Lake in particular
  • LA community: Strong Australian community in Venice Beach area

🇧🇷 Brazilian Visitors

  • Entry: Verify current requirements at travel.state.gov — entry rules for Brazilian citizens have been subject to policy changes. Do not assume — check before booking.
  • Language: Portuguese-speaking services available in some hotels; Spanish widely spoken
  • LA community: Brazilian community concentrated in areas of Westwood and West LA
  • Soccer events: Rose Bowl soccer will have strong Brazilian fan presence

🇨🇳 Chinese Visitors

  • Entry: US visa required — apply via the US Embassy in China well in advance of travel
  • Language: Mandarin and Cantonese widely spoken in Chinatown (DTLA) and the San Gabriel Valley
  • Food: San Gabriel Valley (Alhambra, Monterey Park) has the best Chinese food outside China
  • Payment: Major cards accepted everywhere — carry USD backup

🇩🇪 German Visitors

  • Entry: ESTA required — apply online before travel
  • Time zone: LA is 9 hours behind Berlin (CEST)
  • Beer: No biergartens, but Culver City has a strong craft beer scene
  • LA community: German speakers common in tech and film industry circles

🇫🇷 French Visitors

  • Entry: ESTA required — apply online before travel
  • Time zone: LA is 9 hours behind Paris (CEST)
  • Food: French restaurants in West Hollywood and Beverly Hills; large French expat community
  • Keyboards: US keyboards are QWERTY — bring your own if needed for work

🇲🇽 Mexican Visitors

  • Entry: US visa required — apply via the US Embassy in Mexico in advance
  • Language: Spanish is widely spoken across LA — particularly in East LA, Boyle Heights, and throughout the San Fernando Valley
  • Community: LA has the largest Mexican-American community outside Mexico — 4M+ residents
  • Food: East LA and Boyle Heights have the most authentic Mexican food in the city

Practical Info for All International Visitors

CurrencyUS Dollar (USD). Major credit and debit cards accepted almost everywhere. ATMs at all venues and most 7-Elevens. Notify your bank before travel.
Power110V / 60Hz. Type A and B plugs. Bring a universal adapter if your devices use other standards.
EmergencyDial 911 for police, fire, and medical. This is a free call from any phone including mobile.
SIM / eSIMT-Mobile and AT&T offer tourist eSIM plans from ~$30/month. Buy before arrival or at LAX. Most international visitors find eSIM the simplest option.
HealthTravel insurance with medical coverage is strongly recommended. US healthcare without insurance is extremely expensive. Hospitals are located near all venue clusters.
Tipping18–20% at sit-down restaurants. $1–2 per drink at bars. $5 per night for hotel housekeeping. Tipping is standard and expected — it is part of how these workers are compensated.
DrivingDrive on the right. International driving licenses are accepted. Speed limits are in miles per hour. Turning right on a red light is generally permitted unless signed otherwise.
Drinking age21 — strictly enforced. ID will be checked at bars, restaurants, and venues. Bring a passport or your country's national ID.
ESTA / VisaVerify requirements for your country at travel.state.gov. ESTA must be applied for online at esta.cbp.dhs.gov.
